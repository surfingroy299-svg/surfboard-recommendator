# Direction 01 — The Shaping Bay

**One sentence:** Your three recommended boards wait in a shaper's bay at
golden hour, and the system walks you through them the way a trusted craftsman
would — board on the stand, work lamp raking across the foam, reasons written
where they belong: on the board.

- **Product metaphor:** the shaper's bay — the one room in surf culture where
  boards are *explained*, not sold.
- **Intended emotional response:** warmth, trust, "someone who knows me built
  this set." Calm confidence, not showroom pressure.
- **Why it fits the product:** the product's core promise is explainability
  from real signals; a craftsman's walkthrough is the cultural template for
  exactly that. The bay stages evidence (Q-derived reasons) as expertise.
- **Why it improves decision-making:** region-anchored explanation (tail note
  *on the tail*, rail note *at the rail* under raking light) binds "why" to
  "what" — the user learns to *see* the difference they're choosing between.
- **Why it is not conventional ecommerce:** there is no grid, no cards, no
  price wall; one board is in your hands at a time, the other two wait on the
  rack like real boards do; progression is a conversation, not a filter query.
- **Experience-before-technology order followed:** metaphor → decision value →
  spatial behavior → interaction → comparison → 3D value check → tech. 3D adds
  value here because raking light and rotation communicate rail/foil *form
  impressions* (labeled approximations) that flat images cannot, and the room
  itself performs trust.
- **Territory:** warm / spatial / intuitive.
- **Harvested from concept field:** S9's guided intro (first board walkthrough
  with camera-synced notes) becomes this direction's entry moment.
